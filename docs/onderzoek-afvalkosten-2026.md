## Implicaties voor ons (analyse TARS)

1. **⭐ Ons BSA-tarief van €120/ton is fors ONDER de markt.** Benchmark: Amsterdam Recyclepunt bedrijven €225,75/ton; marktbandbreedte gemengd BSA €170–270/ton. De strategie "heronderhandelen op tarief" is daarmee van tafel — het doel wordt: **€120 zo lang mogelijk vasthouden** (schriftelijke tarievenkaart 25 feb is goud waard in het dispuut), terwijl we volume UIT die stroom halen via scheiding en hergebruik.
2. **Belastingklif 2028:** afvalstoffenbelasting €40,85 (2026) → €90,21/ton (2028) + AVI CO₂-heffing v.a. 2026. Elke ton die we nu leren scheiden wordt elk jaar meer waard.
3. **Matrassen: MRN vergoedt aangesloten inzamelaars 100%** (containerhuur+transport+verwerking, zonder minimumvolume). Bevestigt de route in docs/mail-mrn-matrassen.md → mail afmaken en versturen.
4. **België/Duitsland: NIET doen voor gemengd afval.** Gate fees vergelijkbaar (€150–300/ton), transport kost €13–40/ton, en de afvalstoffenbelasting geldt óók bij export voor verwijdering. Alleen interessant voor schone monostromen (metaal, hard plastic) via EVOA Annex VII — en dan nog alleen bij een concrete koper.
5. **Opbrengststromen:** metaal (koper €5,70–7,20/kg), AEEA vaak netto-positief via Stichting OPEN-verwerkers, textiel €0,20–0,30/kg mits schoon/droog.
6. **Contract:** VNA-voorwaarden/NEA-index is de norm; bij nieuwe afspraken max 24–36 mnd, indexatieplafond, stroomniveau-prijzen, benchmarkclausule, geen exclusiviteit.
7. **VIHB-registratie (NIWO)** nodig zodra we andermans afval vervoeren — checken of eigen-bedrijfsafval-vrijstelling ons dekt bij de huidige werkwijze.

## Rapport (integraal)

# Waste Disposal Costs & Optimization for a Dutch Property-Clearance Business (2025–2026)

## TL;DR
- **Stop paying to burn what you can sell or separate.** Mixed commercial/construction waste (BSA/grof restafval) costs roughly €170–270/ton to dispose of in the Netherlands, while separated streams are far cheaper (A-hout ~€35/ton, schoon puin €7,50–15/ton) and metals/electronics can be net revenue. Every ton diverted from mixed disposal saves €150–250.
- **The Dutch tax clock is the reason to act now:** afvalstoffenbelasting on incineration/landfill is €39,70/ton (2025) and €40,85/ton (2026), but rises to €90,21/ton in 2028 and €113,81/ton by 2035 (2025 price level), plus a new AVI CO₂-heffing from 2026 — so mixed-disposal gate fees will climb steeply. Separation and reuse channels (RetourMatras 100% reimbursed, textile, scrap metal, kringloop) protect you from this.
- **Cross-border export is legally feasible but rarely worth it for a small operator:** Belgium and Germany mixed-waste gate fees are broadly comparable (€150–300/ton), so a 150–250 km trip (~€1,10–2,00/km, i.e. €330–1,000 for a round trip) usually eats the differential. Export mainly pays for clean, high-value mono-streams (metals, hard plastics) under the simplified EVOA Annex VII "green list" procedure.

## Key Findings

1. **Mixed disposal is the expensive default; separation is the lever.** In the Amsterdam region, a business self-delivering mixed grof afval + BSA to the gemeente Amsterdam Recyclepunt voor bedrijven pays €225,75 per 1.000 kg (excl. btw). Comparable Dutch processor price lists cluster at €185–270/ton for BSA (sorteerbaar ~€175–190; niet-sorteerbaar ~€230–270) and €170–190/ton for gemengd bedrijfsafval/restafval. Separated streams are dramatically cheaper: A-hout ~€35/ton, B-hout ~€70/ton, schoon puin €7,50–15/ton.

2. **Some streams are free or revenue-positive.** Mattresses are reimbursed 100% via Matras Recycling Nederland (MRN); scrap metal earns ~€0,13–0,22/kg for iron and far more for non-ferrous (copper ~€5,70–7,20/kg, aluminium ~€0,85–1,60/kg); AEEA/e-waste is now often net-positive for businesses because processors receive a Stichting OPEN reimbursement and pay for the recoverable metals; textiles fetch ~€0,20–0,30/kg when clean and dry.

3. **The regulatory tailwind is powerful.** The afvalstoffenbelasting more than doubles by 2028 and the AVI CO₂-heffing arrives in 2026, both pushing mixed-disposal gate fees up. This makes every euro invested in on-site separation and reuse channels increasingly valuable.

4. **Export is a niche tool, not a core strategy.** EVOA green-list (Annex VII) shipments need only a completed Annex VII form plus a contract — no notification — for clean mono-streams to OECD/EU destinations. But transport economics and the coming digital DIWASS system (from 21 May 2026) mean export only pays for clean, dense, high-value materials.

## Details

### 1. Current Dutch tariffs (2025–2026)

**Waste tax (afvalstoffenbelasting), the key cost driver.** One rate applies to landfill, incineration, and export of waste for disposal ("Voor het storten en verbranden van afvalstoffen en het overbrengen van afvalstoffen buiten Nederland geldt 1 tarief per 1.000 kg", Belastingdienst): €39,23/ton (2024) → €39,70/ton (2025) → €40,85/ton (2026). Under the Belastingplan 2026 Memorie van Toelichting it rises "van € 39,70 per ton afval naar € 90,21 per ton afval in 2028 tot structureel € 113,81 per ton afval vanaf 2035 (prijspeil 2025)". A separate storten-met-ontheffing rate of €234,83/ton starts in 2029. From 2026 a national CO₂-heffing applies to waste incineration (AVIs), with the AVI tariff climbing "tot €295 per ton CO2 in 2030" (the vrijstelling is phased to nil between 2030 and 2033); Renewi's Marktontwikkelingen 2026 cites indicative pass-through figures of ~€6,50/ton for restafval and ~€8,50/ton for hazardous waste. Net effect: mixed-disposal gate fees will rise materially every year.

**Gate fees (poorttarieven) per ton — benchmark table.** Named processors in Amsterdam/North-Holland (Sortiva in Beverwijk/Heemskerk, Renewi/Icova at Siciliëweg 38 Amsterdam Westpoort and Kajuitweg, Meerlanden in Rijsenhout) largely quote by phone and do not publish open €/ton gate fees. The strongest published in-region figure is the gemeente Amsterdam Recyclepunt voor bedrijven at €225,75 per 1.000 kg (excl. btw) for grof afval + bouw- en sloopafval. Published price lists from comparable Dutch processors (Zagron, Avri, Sneek Recycling) give the stream-by-stream structure the Amsterdam market broadly mirrors:

**Hard plastics.** Clean, sorted hard plastics have positive or near-zero value: deAfvalMarkt quotes mono-PP (clean) at +€100/ton (revenue to supplier) and mixed plastics at €0/ton; Van Werven (recyclingplastics.nl) processes ~150 million kg/year of post-consumer hard plastics (garden furniture, crates, pipes) into secondary raw material. The lesson: hard plastics only pay if separated clean; mixed-in plastic just reclassifies your load as more expensive BSA.

**Mattresses.** Retail take-back is €24,95 (single) / €34,95 (double); the producer afvalbeheerbijdrage is €6,50 (single) / €7,50 (double) in 2025, rising to €7,50 / €9,00 in 2026. Critically, **registered collectors are reimbursed 100%** of container, transport and processing costs by MRN — "Ook in 2026 is de vergoeding van de kosten van containerhuur, transport en verwerking 100%" (mrn.nl), up from 15% in 2022, 60% in 2023, and 100% from 2024 — provided mattresses are dry and clean and delivered via an approved recycler.

**Metals — revenue stream.** Scrap iron ~€0,13–0,22/kg depending on grade (average ~€0,205/kg in 2025); RVS ~€0,70/kg; aluminium ~€0,85–1,60/kg; lead ~€1,10–1,30/kg; brass ~€3,80–4,30/kg; red copper ~€5,70–6,10/kg; stripped copper ~€6,80–7,20/kg. Many buyers (Oudijzermarkt, Doornbos Metals, Holland Recycling) collect free and pay day-price by weight.

**AEEA / e-waste — often revenue-positive.** Because processors like Van Gerrevink receive a Stichting OPEN reimbursement and recover gold, tin, platinum, copper and steel, businesses can now be paid for valuable devices (white goods, cabling) while paying only for hazardous streams (fridges, monitors). Van Gerrevink and Schenk Recycling (a Wecycle point) both pay day-price on recoverable metals. Note: businesses are legally the "ontdoener" responsible for proper processing, so use a Cenelec/WEEELABEX-certified processor.

**Textiles.** Since 2025, separate textile collection is mandatory and under UPV. Clean, dry, wearable textile earns ~€0,20–0,30/kg (Texcollect: €0,20 for 50–200 kg, €0,25 for 200–500 kg, €0,30 above 500 kg). But the ketenwide picture is a net deficit: the 2026 actualised cost prices (Stichting UPV Textiel) show inname/opschoning at €0,07–0,12/kg net shortfall and sortering €0,07–0,14/kg shortfall, so pricing is fragile and quality-dependent.

### 2. Belgium and Germany waste market

**Belgium (Antwerp/Ghent).** Businesses must use an OVAM-registered inzamelaar or licensed verwerker. Named players: IVAGO (Ghent municipal commercial waste), Van Gansewinkel/Renewi (Puurs), Vanheede Environment Group, IVAREM (recyclageparken Duffel/Lier/Mechelen). IVAREM business recyclagepark tariffs (€/kg): harde plastic €1,07; grofvuil €0,75; sloophout €0,18; recycleerbaar bouwafval €0,11; grond €0,28; PVC €0,30; glas €0,10 — while AEEA, metals (ferro/non-ferro), matrassen, textiel, papier/karton, PMD are free. Flemish restafval processing heffing is doubling (Ghent: €16→€32/ton), pushing 2026 municipal tariffs up ~15% for restafval and ~7% for other streams. Belgian container-market indications: zuiver steenpuin €20–25/ton, houtafval €50–120/ton, gemengd bouwafval €80–150/ton.

**Germany (NRW / Lower Saxony).** Mixed commercial waste (gemischte Gewerbeabfälle) via container runs ~€370 (5 m³) to €590 (10 m³) all-in; Baumischabfall €150–300/ton; reiner Bauschutt €30–80/ton; incineration price (Beseitigung) at municipal plants like AWG Wuppertal is €243,95/ton. Container-service mixed waste generally €150–700 depending on size. So German mixed-waste disposal is broadly comparable to Dutch levels — not a systematic bargain.

**Dutch waste tax export implication.** The afvalstoffenbelasting explicitly applies to "het overbrengen van afvalstoffen buiten Nederland" for disposal — i.e. exporting waste for disposal (not recovery) is taxed like domestic disposal, removing the tax-arbitrage incentive. Export only makes economic sense for recovery (nuttige toepassing) of materials with genuine market value.

**Transport economics.** A tractor-trailer costs ~€1,10–2,00/km all-in (Panteia/industry figures ~€1,75–2,07/km for trekker+oplegger). A 150–250 km one-way trip is therefore ~€330–1,000 round trip. From 1 July 2026 the vrachtwagenheffing adds cost: per TLN, "voor een Euro 6 voertuig met een technische maximummassa (van de combinatie) van meer dan 32 ton zal het tarief 20,1 eurocent per kilometer bedragen (prijspeil 2026)", with an average tariff of ~€0,191/km (a temporary ~22% discount applies Sep–Dec 2026). Spread over a ~25-ton load, base transport is ~€13–40/ton — meaning a destination must be at least that much cheaper per ton just to break even, before administrative cost. For mixed waste with near-identical cross-border gate fees, the trip rarely pays.

### 3. Legal / regulatory for waste export (EVOA)

- **Two regimes:** Green-list waste for recovery uses the simplified **Annex VII (Bijlage VII)** information procedure — a completed Annex VII form plus a contract between consignor and consignee travel with the load; no prior authority approval. Orange-list and unlisted waste require the full **notification (kennisgeving)** procedure with approval from competent authorities in both countries, which can take months.
- **What qualifies as green list:** clean mono-streams (assessed against EVOA Annex III + Annex V part I list B). Mixed or contaminated streams typically fall out of green list into notification or export bans.
- **Major 2026 changes:** From **21 May 2026** notifications and Annex VII transports must be filed via the EU's digital **DIWASS** system — per ILT, "Vanaf 21 mei 2026 ... U kunt vanaf dan geen gebruik meer maken van MijnILT of de DNA Tool". Because a notification can take months, ILT advises "voor 21 februari een aanvraag te doen, maar zeker niet later dan 31 maart 2026" for shipments needing approval. The obligation to log Annex VII data in DIWASS was postponed to **1 January 2027**.
- **Domestic registration — VIHB.** Any business that transports, collects, trades or brokers commercial/hazardous waste in the Netherlands must be on the NIWO VIHB list (V/I/H/B letters denote roles). Requirements: reliability (VOG NP or RP) and professional competence (vakdiploma Afvalstoffen), applied via eHerkenning (EH3+). Waste moved solely to/from your own business is exempt; pure logistics brokering needs no VIHB (the carrier does). Belgium requires OVAM registration; Germany requires Anzeige/Erlaubnis under AbfAEV.

### 4. Alternative sales channels (avoid disposal)

- **Mattresses — RetourMatras / MRN:** RetourMatras operates four NL recycling plants (Lelystad, Alphen aan den Rijn, Zeeland NB, Etten-Leur) plus HQ in Bodegraven, with ~1,5 million mattresses/year capacity; the other approved recycler is Matras Recycling Europe. Register on the MRN portal, contract with an approved recycler, and reclaim 100% of costs. This turns a former cost stream into a net-zero one — the single biggest quick win for a clearance business.
- **Kringloop / reuse operators:** Branchevereniging Kringloop Nederland (BKN, founded 1994) is the umbrella. Rataplan (~50 stores; collects ~8,1 million kg/year, ~47% resold, ~33% material reuse) and Het Goed (~32 million kg collected, 88% reuse) are the largest. They accept furniture, appliances, textiles, books, electronics — free diversion of saleable goods with social/CO₂ benefit. The branch achieves ~48% reuse and ~36% recycling of intake.
- **Textile collection:** clean/dry/wearable ~€0,20–0,30/kg (Texcollect); mandatory separate collection since 2025 under UPV Textiel with erkende dienstverleners contracted via Stichting UPV Textiel; koepels VHT and BKN. Fragile economics — quality is everything.
- **Scrap metal buyers:** Oudijzermarkt, Doornbos Metals, Holland Recycling, MIBO, van Pelt Recycling — free collection, day-price payment. Non-ferrous (copper, brass, aluminium) is where the money is.
- **Secondhand goods exporters:** Direct Ontruimen (20+ years furniture export), plus opkopers HD Trade, Gideon Italiaander, Nexttrade buy whole inboedels/business inventories; shippers (Sobou, BSI Shipping) move containers Rotterdam→West/Central Africa. These absorb bulk volumes of resaleable furniture/goods that would otherwise be BSA.

### 5. Contract strategy (Amsterdam region, 2026)

- **Market rate:** For mixed BSA/grof restafval with self-delivery, budget €170–270/ton depending on sorteerbaarheid; the Amsterdam city Recyclepunt reference is €225,75/ton excl. btw. Renewi (national market leader), Icova (Amsterdam), Sortiva (Beverwijk), and Meerlanden (Rijsenhout) are the main competitive processors; most quote per tender, so get 3+ quotes.
- **Indexation:** The Dutch industry standard is the **NEA-index** (Panteia "Kostenontwikkelingen in het wegvervoer") for the transport component, supplemented by CBS inflation/CPI and pass-through of afvalstoffenbelasting increases. Named processors confirm this: Avri (NEA + CBS), Nijhuis Milieu Service ("het recht om jaarlijks haar prijzen te verhogen conform het NEA-indexcijfer"), De Graaf Groep, Renewi. The sector-standard VNA algemene voorwaarden 2019 grant broad cost pass-through rights.
- **Watch-outs:** Default contract terms are long (Nijhuis: 60 months / 5 years). Small operators should push for shorter terms, a cap on annual indexation, transparent stream-level pricing (not blended), and a benchmarking clause allowing re-tender if market rates fall.

## Recommendations

**Stage 1 — Immediate (0–3 months), no capital:**
1. **Register with MRN and contract an approved mattress recycler (RetourMatras or Matras Recycling Europe).** Mattresses move from a cost to 100% reimbursed. Highest ROI, zero downside.
2. **Set up on-site separation** of the five high-value/low-cost streams: metals (revenue), AEEA (often revenue), A/B-hout (~€35–70/ton vs €225+/ton mixed), clean puin (€7,50–15/ton), and resaleable goods (free to kringloop/opkoper). Target: keep mixed BSA below 40% of tonnage.
3. **Get a VIHB registration** via NIWO if you transport waste yourself — it is a prerequisite for most of the above and for any export.

**Stage 2 — Contract optimization (3–6 months):**
4. **Re-tender your mixed-waste contract** with 3+ quotes (Renewi, Icova, Sortiva, Meerlanden). Insist on stream-level pricing, NEA-index-only indexation with an annual cap (e.g. max 5%), a term no longer than 24–36 months, and a benchmarking/re-open clause.
5. **Build standing offtake relationships** with a scrap-metal buyer (day-price), a kringloop operator (Het Goed/Rataplan), and a secondhand exporter/opkoper for bulk furniture.

**Stage 3 — Strategic (6–18 months):**
6. **Model the 2028 tax cliff now.** At €90,21/ton waste tax (plus CO₂-heffing), mixed-disposal gate fees could approach €300/ton. Every percentage point of separation you build now compounds in value. Reassess whether investing in a small sorting area or baler pays back.
7. **Only pursue export for clean mono-streams** (metals, hard plastics) under Annex VII, and only when a specific buyer offers a per-ton price that beats the domestic net (gate fee saved + material value) by more than the ~€13–40/ton transport cost.

**Benchmarks that change the plan:**
- If a domestic processor offers mixed BSA below ~€160/ton with stream-level transparency, lock it in.
- If scrap/copper prices fall >30%, deprioritize metal-handling labor.
- If cross-border gate fees diverge by >€60/ton for a clean stream, revisit export.
- When the 2028 tax step is confirmed in-force, escalate separation capex.

## Caveats
- **In-region gate fees are largely non-public.** Sortiva, Renewi/Icova Amsterdam, and Meerlanden quote per tender; the €/ton figures above are anchored on the published gemeente Amsterdam rate (€225,75/ton) plus out-of-region processor price lists (Zagron, Avri, Sneek) used as structural benchmarks. Validate with direct quotes before contracting.
- **Metal and plastic prices are volatile day-prices** driven by world markets; treat all €/kg figures as indicative snapshots (mostly 2025).
- **Textile economics are in structural deficit** ketenwide; per-kg revenue only holds for clean, dry, wearable material and could disappear as UPV Textiel reshapes the market.
- **One RetourMatras page cites three (not four) recycling locations** amid a possible "RetourMat" rebrand; four is the well-documented figure.
- **Belgian/German figures mix municipal recyclagepark rates, container all-in prices, and per-ton gate fees** — not always like-for-like with Dutch self-delivery gate fees; use for order-of-magnitude comparison only.
- **The vrachtwagenheffing and DIWASS timelines** are as announced for 2026; confirm exact rates and go-live before building them into contracts.
